# 10 — Roadmap and task board

Phases are ordered so that something runs end to end as early as possible (decision D21) and each later phase widens one axis: rules coverage, card coverage, agent strength, or operability. Effort is given in rough "focused sessions" (a session ≈ a few hours of AI-assisted work) and is deliberately conservative; the rules engine and parser are where estimates are least reliable.

Status legend: `[ ]` todo · `[~]` in progress · `[x]` done.

## Phase 1 — Engine core (≈ 10–14 sessions)

- [x] 1.1 State model, object ids, zones, seeded RNG, structural-sharing update helper, event emitter. *(Zone and state shape settled in ADR 0002. `GameState` gains its per-subsystem fields in 1.4/1.6/1.8/1.9.)*
- [x] 1.2 Turn structure with all steps; untap/draw/cleanup; land drops; turn cap. *(Also extra turns. The first-strike damage step is skipped until combat exists in 1.6. Cleanup's discard-to-hand-size is a real player choice, so it takes an injected `chooseDiscards` hook for now and fails loudly without one; 1.4 replaces the hook with a `pendingDecision`. Timing and land-drop limits are enforced; "is this card actually a land" waits on card definitions in 2.1.)*
- [x] 1.3 Mana: pool, costs (incl. hybrid/phyrexian/X), payment solver, mana abilities of basic and simple nonbasic lands. *(Pool reshaped to carry snow and spend restrictions per unit, and phyrexian mana folded into hybrid; see ADR 0003. The solver is exact, including {S} and {X}. Spend restrictions are carried but enforced in 1.4, when the engine knows what is being cast. `ManaAbility` is modelled and resolves without the stack; wiring real lands' abilities waits on card scripts in 2.1.)*
- [x] 1.4 Priority, stack, casting/activating, resolution, countering, fizzling, split second. *(The `step(state, decision)` model from docs/01 is live: `pendingDecision`, `applyDecision`, and the priority loop, which also retires 1.2's `chooseDiscards` hook. Stack resolution is LIFO, with countering and split second. Two parts wait on their own phases: a priority decision only offers `pass` until `legalActions` enumerates the rest in 1.5, and fizzling needs targets, also 1.5. Checking state-based actions and putting triggers on the stack sit immediately before priority (CR 117.5) and arrive in 1.7 and 1.8; abilities as objects on the stack need card scripts in 2.1.)*
- [x] 1.5 Targeting and legality (`legalActions`), hexproof/shroud/protection/ward. *(Shroud stops everyone, hexproof only opponents, protection is colour-based, and ward is reported as a cost rather than a targeting restriction. Fizzling (CR 608.2b) lands here too, as 1.4 flagged. Keywords are stored on the object for now and become the per-object cache `characteristics()` fills in 1.9. `legalActions` covers timing and payability — including mana untapped lands could still make — and reads what a card *is* through a `CardInfoSource` that 2.1 implements over card scripts; wiring it into the priority decision's options needs those same definitions.)*
- [x] 1.6 Combat with all evergreen keywords, damage assignment, first-strike step. *(Attack and block declaration with flying/reach/menace/vigilance/haste/defender, damage-assignment order, first and double strike, trample, deathtouch, lifelink. Damage is applied as one batch so creatures trade. Creatures die in 1.7, when state-based actions arrive; indestructible is carried for it. Damage assignment picks the canonical legal split (lethal down the order, excess to the last blocker or trampled over) — a free-form `assignDamage` decision is a refinement, not a correctness fix. Power/toughness joins keywords as stored-for-now, computed in 1.9.)*
- [x] 1.7 State-based actions, legend rule, counters, tokens. *(CR 704: losing at zero life, to decking and to poison; lethal damage, zero toughness and deathtouch, with indestructible sparing the first two but not zero toughness; +1/+1 and -1/-1 annihilation; tokens ceasing to exist; auras dying and equipment falling off; planeswalker loyalty. The legend rule asks the controller which to keep rather than choosing for them. Checked whenever a player would receive priority and repeated until nothing applies. `characteristics.ts` applies counters to power and toughness — the first slice of the 1.9 layer system, which subsumes it.)*
- [x] 1.8 Triggered abilities (APNAP, intervening-if, LKI, delayed), static abilities. *(Triggers fire on their event, wait, and go on the stack before the next priority in APNAP order, with the controller choosing among their own. Intervening-if is checked as it would go on the stack; last known information is captured before the source leaves, so dies triggers work. Delayed and once-each-turn triggers included. Abilities on the stack cease to exist on resolution rather than going to a graveyard. **Static abilities are not here**: they are continuous effects, and they only mean anything once the layer system exists, so they move to 1.9.)*
- [x] 1.9 Continuous effects and the layer system with timestamps and dependency. *(Layers 1-7 with all five of layer 7's sublayers, timestamp order within a layer, dependency ordering per CR 613.8 with a timestamp fallback on cycles, durations including until-end-of-turn and while-source-on-battlefield, and per-state memoisation. Every characteristic reader in the engine goes through `characteristics(state, id)`; the values on `GameObject` are printed values only. Dependency detection covers "changes what an effect applies to", which is complete for the current effect vocabulary because no change reads the board — `dependsOn` is the one place to widen when 2.1 adds one that does. The named cards in docs/09 (Humility + Opalescence, Blood Moon + Urza lands, Painter's Servant) are tested by shape here and by name once card scripts exist. Static abilities moved here from 1.8 and are continuous effects with a `whileSourceOnBattlefield` duration.)*

- [x] 1.10 Replacement and prevention effects. *(CR 614-616. The engine no longer changes state directly: it builds `RulesEvent`s and hands them to `runBatch`, which runs each past every applicable replacement before `performEvents` applies what survives — see ADR 0004. Self-replacement first (616.1a), never twice to the same event (614.5), and the affected player chooses when several still apply, which is a real `chooseReplacement` decision and so can pause a batch mid-flight. Prevention (615) is a species of replacement, with shields that shrink by what they absorb; regeneration (701.15) is a one-use shield over destruction specifically, which is why zero toughness — being put into the graveyard rather than destroyed — is not saved by one. Combat damage, drawing, discarding, dying, the legend rule, spell resolution, fizzling and countering all go through the pipeline, so lifelink's life gain is itself replaceable. The effect vocabulary is what the current engine can produce; card scripts in 2.1 add to it, and `performEvents` is where the docs/03 effect ops hang.)*
- [x] 1.11 Planeswalkers. *(CR 306 and 606. A planeswalker enters with loyalty counters equal to its printed loyalty, seeded into 1.10's `entersBattlefield` event rather than set afterwards — which is what lets a Doubling Season double it, since that is a replacement effect. Damage dealt to one removes that many loyalty counters instead of being marked (CR 306.8); a permanent that is both a creature and a planeswalker gets both. Attackers choose a defender per attacker from the defending player and the planeswalkers they control (CR 508.1a), and the declaration is validated against that list. Trample over blockers already went to "the player or planeswalker it's attacking" (CR 702.19b), so that needed a test rather than a change; damage aimed at a player is not redirected, the 2018 rules change. Loyalty abilities are activated at sorcery speed, once per permanent per turn (CR 606.3), with the counters paid as a cost — so Doubling Season does **not** double a `+2`, and an ultimate that empties a planeswalker kills it to a state-based action while its ability is still on the stack and resolves anyway. `legalActions` offers them and `whyNotActivateLoyalty` is the single legality check both it and activation use, so the engine never rejects what it offered. What each ability *does* is a card script, roadmap 2.1.)*
- [x] 1.12 London mulligan; game end conditions; loop detection. *(`setUpGame` shuffles, deals seven each and runs the mulligan rounds: both players declare in turn order before any mulligan is taken, those who mulligan redraw a fresh seven together, and on keeping a player puts one card under their library per mulligan taken (CR 103.4b) — the London mulligan is not "draw one fewer". `startGame` still skips all of it for tests and for 1.13's scenario builder. `game-end.ts` is now the only thing that writes `state.result`: conceding, effects that make a player win or lose outright, the turn cap, and the two draws we impose ourselves. Loop detection (CR 726) hashes a canonical projection of the state at every decision point — a repeat within a turn is a draw — and a decision cap backstops the loops the hash cannot see, such as one that shuffles. The hash is 53 bits across two FNV passes rather than 32, because a collision here is a game silently called a draw rather than a crash.)*
- [x] 1.13 Scenario builder for tests; unit suites for 1.2–1.12; invariant fuzzer with the `random` agent. *(All three live behind `@mtg/engine/testing`, a second entry point, so the runtime bundle keeps its zero dependencies and its size. The scenario builder has the fluent shape docs/09 sketches but describes permanents by what the engine can read off them rather than by card name — names arrive with card scripts in 2.1, and `name` is a label until then, which the legend rule happens to read. The `random` agent answers every decision kind uniformly from an injected generator, so a failing game replays from its seed. The fuzzer asserts the docs/09 invariants after **every** decision and checks `replay(decisions) == state`; the stronger `replay(log) == state` needs an event-log consumer that does not exist yet. The unit suites were audited against the eleven bullets in docs/09 rather than padded: they were already substantively there. Two of those bullets are blocked rather than missing — "changing targets" and additional/alternative costs need card effects in 2.1.)*
- [x] 1.14 Benchmarks; target ≤ 5 ms/game with the random agent. *(`packages/engine/bench`, run with `pnpm bench`: fixed-seed games in four cases — a baseline board, a wide board, a long game, and the baseline without loop detection so its cost is measured rather than guessed — reporting ms/game, games/sec and decisions/sec. **Median 2.2 ms a game**, inside the target. It did not start there: the first run was 23 ms, nine tenths of it CR 726 loop detection hashing a full position at every one of ~510 decision points. Chasing that found a rules bug the unit tests had not — the projection ignored `state.combat`, so ordering blockers on one attacker looked like a position repeating and **36% of wide-board games were ending as false draws**. Both halves are fixed, with tests and ADR 0005: the hash covers combat, the paused replacement batch and the triggers fired this turn, and it only watches a turn once that turn has run longer than any ordinary turn, since a loop never stops being one. CI runs the benchmarks and fails on a regression greater than 20% against the last run on `main`. `packages/agents/bench` and the ≤ 50 ms search-AI figure wait on agents in phase 4; a benchmark over real games waits on cards in 2.1.)*

## Phase 2 — Cards: schema, bootstrap set, resolver (≈ 5–7 sessions)

- [x] 2.1 Card-script zod schema + JSON Schema export; loader that turns a script into an engine `CardDefinition`; effect-op registry in the engine (first ~40 ops). *(The engine gained a card vocabulary — filters, quantities, selectors and conditions, all of them data — and **39 effect ops**, each implemented once and each proposing `RulesEvent`s so replacement effects apply without either side knowing about the other. Definitions live in `GameState` and objects take their printed characteristics and abilities from them; static and replacement abilities are *derived* from what is on the battlefield rather than registered and unregistered, so a blink or a control change needs no special case (ADR 0006). Casting is now the rest of CR 601.2 — timing, targets checked against what the ability asks for, and paying, with sources tapped in object order when the pool is short — and resolution runs the card's effects before the stack finishes the move. `@mtg/cards` has the zod schema for the friendly YAML forms docs/03 shows, the JSON Schema export (`pnpm cards:schema`, checked in CI), and a loader that converts and checks: every op exists, has the arguments it needs and none it does not, every `$target` was declared, and every `when`/`affects`/`change` names something the engine really has — all against the engine's own exported lists, so the two cannot drift. **Deliberately left:** ops that need a player choice mid-resolution (search, scry, may, modal, a chosen discard) need the effect list to pause and resume as a replacement batch does; until that exists a batch that pauses mid-effects throws rather than dropping the rest. Dynamic P/T (`*`/`*+1`) needs quantity-valued layer changes. Two bugs the first end-to-end test found are fixed here: triggers waited a full priority round because resolution granted priority directly instead of returning to `advanceToDecision` (CR 117.5), and the auto-tapper logged taps it discarded while trying a dual land's modes. A module cycle between `targeting` and `characteristics` broke real entry points depending on load order, and the keyword set moved to a module of its own to break it.)*
- [x] 2.2 Validator: schema, characteristic agreement with Scryfall, text coverage, executability smoke. *(`validateScript(script, card)` returns supported / partial / unsupported with the reasons and the validator version, which is what lets 2.4 cache a verdict and still re-earn it when the validator changes. Characteristic agreement is exact and makes a mismatch *unsupported* rather than a warning — a script that gets a cost wrong is a card quietly better than the one everyone else is playing with. `covers:` moved onto each ability, where docs/03 always had it, and coverage is checked per sentence with reminder text dropped: claimed twice is an error, claimed by nobody is partial. The smoke test plays the card in three synthetic games (empty board, a 2/2 opposite, the opponent's turn) with enough any-colour lands that cost is never the obstacle, and requires the engine to come out in a legal, answerable state without throwing; a scenario with no legal target is skipped rather than failed, because having no target is a normal fact about Magic. The Scryfall projection type moved from the fetch script into `@mtg/cards`, so the writer and the validator cannot read different shapes. **Deliberately left:** the per-hand-script test suite docs/09 wants (`packages/cards/test/scripts.test.ts`) needs hand scripts, which is 2.3; activated and loyalty abilities are not smoke-tested yet, only casting is; and a printed `*` power is reported as a disagreement, since dynamic P/T waits on quantity-valued layer changes.)*
- [x] 2.3 Bootstrap hand scripts (~80 cards): basics, shocks/duals/fetches/checklands, one or two cards per effect op and per keyword, a few planeswalkers, a few layer-system cards (Blood Moon, Glorious Anthem, Humility) — chosen to exercise the engine, not to make a deck. *(**60 cards**, not 80, and the shortfall is the point: the missing ones are the cards the vocabulary cannot yet say, listed with their blocking phase in docs/03 rather than quietly dropped — fetchlands and tutors, shocklands, checklands, modal spells, auras, Blood Moon, Bad Moon. Between them the 60 use all seven ability kinds and 26 of the 39 ops. Three are **partial** and named as such: Wrath of God, Swords to Plowshares and Turn to Frog each do less than their card says. `pnpm cards:new` fetches a card, scaffolds the script from the printed characteristics and records the projection in a committed fixture, so `scripts.test.ts` validates all 60 offline in CI. Writing real cards found four engine gaps and fixed them: an activation cost could not sacrifice its source (CR 118.3), a death trigger could not say "another creature **you control**", a loyalty ability could not take targets, and the auto-tapper could not pay a cost with two of the same pip — it picked a mode that finished the cost rather than one that made progress, so `{1}{B}{B}` never got its second black. Also: a bare `~` is null in YAML, which the loader now says out loud.)*
- [x] 2.4 `ScriptResolver` (hand → cached auto → auto-scripter → validate) with the `card_scripts` cache and `unsupported_requests` logging. *(The chain in order, with two of its four links standing in for things that do not exist yet — and both are ports rather than stubs, so what replaces them changes nothing here. The auto-scripter is phase 3: without one the resolver has no third step and everything outside the bootstrap set is unsupported, which is a configuration rather than a hole. The cache and the log are `ScriptStore`, shaped field for field like docs/06's `card_scripts` and `unsupported_requests`; the in-memory implementation runs now and the SQLite one lands with persistence in phase 5. Every verdict is cached, **including unsupported** — re-running a parser and a smoke test for a card that was unplayable an hour ago is exactly what the cache is for — and a verdict is only believed when the version that produced it is the version running now, which is why the row stores it. Partial scripts are never played and are logged like any other card a run could not have, with the run and the context that asked for it, because docs/03 wants hand-scripting effort to follow demand.)*
- [x] 2.5 Scenario tests per bootstrap card; differential test harness. *(**65 scenario tests over all 60 cards**, declared in each card's own YAML under `tests:` and run by `card-tests.test.ts`, which also fails a card that has no test at all. The vocabulary is a board, one action and an expectation, every part of it optional with a sensible default, and the runner reports failures as sentences so one run names every card that broke rather than stopping at the first. Writing tests for real cards found three engine bugs, each fixed with a failing test first: **a land played from hand never entered** — `playLand` moved the object instead of proposing an event, so "this land enters tapped" had nothing to replace (ADR 0004); **a permanent's own "as it enters" replacement was invisible**, because replacements were derived from the battlefield and a card entering is not on it yet (CR 614.12, noted on ADR 0006); and **casting checked the number of targets but never what the spell asks for**, so "destroy target artifact" could be aimed at a creature and "counter target noncreature spell" at a creature spell (CR 601.2c). The last one immediately failed three of the new card tests, which had been written against the hole. The differential harness plays two scripts through the validator's three smoke boards and the card's own tests and compares the whole resulting position rather than the declared expectations, so an auto script that quietly also drew a card is caught by a test nobody wrote. **Deliberately left:** it has nothing real to compare until the auto-scripter exists in phase 3 — what runs today is the harness against deliberately altered copies of a script, plus every bootstrap card compared with itself. The seeded regression games of docs/09 §3 wait on decks and the match runner in phase 5.)*

## Phase 3 — Auto-scripter v1 (≈ 8–12 sessions, then continuous)

- [x] 3.1 Normaliser (name → `~`, reminder text, sentence split, keyword lines, Scryfall keyword cross-check). *(`normaliseCard(card)` in `packages/cards/src/auto/normalise.ts`: the card's own name becomes `~` — the full name first, then the short name a legendary card calls itself by, since replacing the short one first leaves "~ Beleren" behind — and modern self-reference ("this creature", "this land") becomes the same `~`, because it is the same rule printed two ways. Punctuation is settled here rather than in the grammar: a loyalty cost's minus sign is U+2212 and not a hyphen, quotes are typographic, and both dash characters appear. **The sentence numbering is now shared rather than reproduced**: `linesOf` gives each line the numbers `covers:` is checked against, and `sentencesOf` is defined as its flattening, so the auto-scripter's `covers:` and the validator's coverage check cannot disagree. Keyword lines are all-or-nothing — "Flying, protection from red" is an ability, not half a keyword line — and the Scryfall cross-check runs in one direction only, because its `keywords` array mixes keyword abilities with action words (Jace Beleren lists "Mill", which the engine has an op for; an Equipment lists "Equip", which it has not), so the rest come back as `otherKeywords` for 3.2 to judge. The printed-name table that was hidden in `checks.ts` is now one shared table read by both the validator and the normaliser, with a test that every grantable keyword has a printed name. Run over the 60-card fixture, it normalises all of them with no notes. **Deliberately left:** only the front face of a two-faced card is read, and it says so in `notes` rather than pretending; the short name is the one guess in here, and a card whose given name is an ordinary English word would have that word replaced.)*
- [x] 3.2 Sentence classifier (keyword / activated / triggered / static / spell / loyalty / unknown). *(`classifyCard(card)` in `packages/cards/src/auto/classify.ts`, over the lines the normaliser produces. **The categories are the engine's seven ability kinds** — spell, activated, triggered, static, mana, loyalty, replacement — plus `keyword` and `unknown`, rather than the seven docs/03 sketched before the engine had card definitions: `mana` and `replacement` are missing from that list and the engine treats both as their own thing, so keeping it would mean the emitter reads the same line a second time to find out what it really was, and a second reader is where the two disagree (**ADR 0007**; a test asserts the correspondence, so a new ability kind cannot be added without a category). Most of it is the Comprehensive Rules rather than heuristics — CR 603.1 for trigger words, CR 602.1 for cost-colon-effect, CR 605.1a for which of those are mana abilities, CR 606.1 for a loyalty cost, CR 614 for replacement templating, and CR 112.3 for the residual, which makes spell-versus-static a rule rather than a guess. A cost is read all or nothing (CR 601.2h), because a cost read wrongly is a card cheaper than the one printed. The ground truth for the tests is the bootstrap set itself: every sentence a hand script claims, classified and compared against the `kind:` a person wrote, with the validator deciding which sentences are unclaimed because their script is partial. It agrees on all sixty and leaves no line unknown. **Deliberately left:** the residual rule is only as good as the rules above it — a replacement template nobody has taught it is called static rather than unknown, and the differential harness is what would catch that on a card that also has a hand script. Sabotage-checking found a rule with no test behind it — that half a cost is not a cost — which is now tested.)*
- [x] 3.3 PEG grammar v1: costs, targets, quantities, durations, conditions, common effect verbs; anaphora resolution. *(A PEG's shape — ordered choice, backtracking, a rule that either matches or puts the cursor back — **written rather than generated with `peggy`**, because what a rule produces is a piece of script built from a closed vocabulary and that is worth type-checking, and because a rule that is a function can be tested and sabotage-checked on its own (**ADR 0008**). `reader.ts` is the machinery, `phrases.ts` the shared vocabulary (quantities, players, filters, durations, the object phrases), `verbs.ts` the ~20 effect templates, `parse.ts` the entry points — effects, triggers, mana modes, statics, replacements and activation costs. **44 of the bootstrap set's 46 ability lines parse**, and every one of them is loaded through `loadCardScript` in a test, because a parse that produces a script the engine cannot read is worse than one that admits it failed. The two that do not are Humility and Turn to Frog, which want layer changes the script vocabulary cannot say. The unit is the sentence, so a parse can stop at a sentence boundary and still be worth having — the shape of a partial script — but never mid-sentence, because half a sentence is a different card. Sabotage-checking eleven rules found two with nothing behind them: that half a cost is not a cost (`{1}, Discard a card` was read as `{1}`, a card cheaper than the printed one) and, earlier, the binding rollback — without it Giant Growth declared three targets for its one "target creature" and Time Warp seven, because each verb that tried and failed left its declaration behind. **Deliberately left:** "its controller" has no spelling in the script vocabulary, so Swords to Plowshares stops after its first sentence; a leading "Until end of turn," is not read; and the order of the verb list is part of the grammar with nothing enforcing it, which the golden corpus in 3.4 is what properly catches.)*
- [x] 3.4 Emitter + golden corpus (≈ 300 cards spanning the common templates). *(`emitScript(card)` assembles a whole script — characteristics off the printed card, keywords from the normaliser, one ability per classified line — and `autoScripter` makes it **the resolver's third link**, which 2.4 built as a port with nothing behind it. The corpus is **370 real cards** (two core sets, `pnpm cards:corpus`, committed because CI has no network) and `corpus-goldens.json` is what the auto-scripter makes of each, rewritten by `pnpm cards:goldens` and checked by `pnpm check`. It reads **124 supported, 244 partial, 2 unsupported**: the two are the cards with a printed `*` power, and **no card in the corpus is emitted with a characteristic that disagrees with the printed one or makes the engine fall over** — a test asserts both, because a script that does less than the card says is never played while one that says something different would be. The `covers:` rule is the whole safety story: it claims only the sentences the grammar actually read. **The differential harness from 2.5 finally has something to compare** — 56 of the 60 bootstrap cards have both a hand script and a supported auto script, and all 56 agree. It earned its keep on the first run: a card whose spell text runs to two lines was emitted as two spell abilities and the engine resolves only the first, so Twisted Image switched power and toughness and never drew its card (CR 112.3a). The loader now refuses a second spell ability, and refuses a filter object with nothing in it, which used to mean "any permanent" silently — a double-parsed script came out targeting anything at all. **Deliberately left:** the smoke test proves a supported card is legal, not that it is right; only the 56 with hand scripts are checked for that, and the rest rest on the grammar's rules being what they say. `pnpm cards:coverage` over the whole of Scryfall is 3.5.)*
- [x] 3.5 `pnpm cards:coverage` full-Scryfall report with top failing patterns; nightly workflow. *(The whole pipeline over every card Scryfall has, streamed a line at a time, writing `reports/coverage.json` for machines and `reports/coverage.md` for people. **Measured over 34,733 cards: 3,398 supported, 30,333 partial, 884 unsupported, 118 unscripted — 9.8% of all cards, 8.0% of the ones that have rules text, and 13,530 of 76,032 sentences claimed.** That is a long way from 3.6's 25% target, which is what 3.6 is for; the number is reported rather than framed. The score is deliberately not one number: a card with no rules text is supported without anything being read, so those 1,253 are counted apart, and sentences-claimed is reported alongside because a card is partial the moment one sentence is unread. **The useful half is the pattern table** — each unread sentence generalised (numbers and mana symbols are what differ between printings of one template) down to its opening words, ranked by how many sentences share its shape, with an example card. The top of it is a to-do list for 3.6: `enchant creature` (893), `at the beginning of your upkeep` (728), `flash` (627), `equip {M}` (592), `whenever ~ deals combat damage to` (466), `activate only as a sorcery` (415), `choose N —` (393). `nightly.yml` runs it against the day's data and keeps one issue up to date with the table, updated in place rather than commented on, and runs the long fuzz beside it — which needed a budget knob, since the fuzzer played forty games whatever the context and a nightly fuzz job would have run exactly what CI already runs. Neither gates anything. **Running it over every card immediately earned its keep**: the smoke test sizes its board from the card's mana value so that cost is never what stops it, and Gleemax costs `{1000000}`, so the validator set about building a million lands three times and never came back. The board is now capped at twenty and a card that wants more is skipped with a reason — a bug a 370-card corpus of two core sets was never going to find. **Deliberately left:** a pattern is a prefix, so it cannot tell "destroy target creature" from the same template with a qualifier past the sixth word, which is the right trade while the top of the list is this coarse.)*
- [ ] 3.6 Coverage push to ≥ 25% of Scryfall `supported` (vanilla/French-vanilla creatures, burn, pump, simple removal, cantrips, counters, ETB/dies triggers, simple anthems, basic mana rocks/dorks). *(**Not met, and left unticked rather than reframed: 3,793 of 34,733 cards — 10.9%, against a 25% target.** Up from 3,398 (9.8%), with sentences claimed 13,511 → 15,063 of 76,032. The corpus goldens read 138 supported where 3.4 read 124. **The ranking was measuring the wrong thing**, which is the more useful half of what landed: a pattern's `count` is how many sentences share its shape, and working from it puts `enchant creature` at the top with 893 — a template that on its own would finish **zero** cards, because every Aura also prints "enchanted creature gets …" and the engine has no selector for what an Aura is attached to. Each pattern now carries **`finishes`**: how many cards have nothing else left unread, so teaching it is the last obstacle. That column is what `reports/coverage.md` tells a reader to work from, and 3.5's fallout split is what makes it trustworthy. Six templates taught off it, each sabotage-checked: **flash and split second** off a keyword line (fields on the card, not entries in `keywords:`, and the validator lets the field claim the line only when the script sets it); **the filter's adjectives and qualifiers** — "attacking or blocking", "tapped", "untapped", "white or blue", "with flying", "without first strike" — which is the broadest of the six because a filter appears in removal, pump, anthems and wipes alike, and every predicate behind it was already in the engine (CR 115.1); **a second damage clause**, "6 damage to target creature and 2 damage to target player" and "1 damage to each creature and each player", which was the commonest thing between a burn spell and being read; **"add one mana of any color"** as the five colours in five modes (CR 105.1 — colourless is not one); **"~ enters with four +1/+1 counters on it"** as the self-replacement it is (CR 614.1c); and **"activate only as a sorcery"** as the `sorceryOnly` an activated ability already has. Teaching the step triggers **whose** step they are found a latent hole: the engine fires one on `whose === 'any' || controller === activePlayer`, so a script that left `whose` out got "your upkeep" by accident and the type requiring it never saw the object — an "each player's upkeep" ability would have triggered half as often as the card printed. The loader now refuses a step trigger without it. **What stands between 10.9% and 25% is named rather than half-done, and it is mostly engine work**: an `attachedTo` effect selector for Auras and Equipment (the four largest shapes, ~2,400 sentences, and 0 `finishes` between them until it exists), a combat-damage trigger (466), modal spells and the mid-resolution choice that search, scry and "you may" also wait on (393), additional casting costs (317) and cost reduction (224), and the cost-carrying keywords — cycling (305), kicker (202), crew (190), flashback (189), morph (141), ward (93), equip (592). "Activate only once each turn" is the smallest of them: a triggered ability has `onceEachTurn` and an activated one does not. The largest remaining grammar bucket, `at the beginning of your upkeep` at 279 `finishes`, is **not one template**: the trigger reads, and the bodies are 279 different effects. Phase 3 is "then continuous" and phase 7 widens by the coverage page, so this is where the rest of it lives.)*

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Rules engine bugs skew evolution silently | Layered tests (09), invariant fuzzing on every push, regression fixtures, sanity ladders. |
| Auto-scripter coverage stalls on the long tail | Random-deck design tolerates gaps; effort is steered by *requested* unsupported cards, not by raw count; hand scripts remain first-class. |
| Layer system + planeswalkers in v1 is heavy | Scheduled last in phase 1 with thorough tests; the thin slice still runs if 1.9/1.11 slip, because random decks re-roll cards that need them. |
| Evaluator quality caps how meaningful "winning" is | Tuning harness; ladders; deeper search available for tiebreaks; LLM coach later. |
| Slow simulations make cycles take hours | Benchmarks in CI; worker pool; `agentLevel` and `trialTopK` knobs. |
| SQLite growth over month-long runs | Compressed logs, retention setting, export as backup. |
| Scryfall rate limits / offline homelab | Bulk data is offline; images are lazy and cached; text-only mode exists. |

## Working agreement

- Every phase ends with its tests green in CI and a short ADR (`docs/adr/NNNN-title.md`) for any decision that changed from these docs.
- Deck lists, bans, and settings are always data; never hard-code a card name in engine or agent code (card names appear only in scripts and tests).
- Determinism is non-negotiable: any new source of randomness must draw from the injected RNG.
